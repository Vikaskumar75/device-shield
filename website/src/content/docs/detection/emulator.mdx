---
title: Emulator & simulator detection
description: Detect Android emulators and the iOS Simulator with device_shield.
---

```dart
final result = await DeviceShield.checkEmulator();
```

## Android signals

| Signal | Condition |
|---|---|
| `fingerprint` | `Build.FINGERPRINT` starts with `generic` or `unknown` |
| `model` | `Build.MODEL` contains `google_sdk`, `Emulator` or `Android SDK built for` |
| `manufacturer` | `Build.MANUFACTURER` contains `Genymotion` |
| `hardware` | `Build.HARDWARE` contains `goldfish`, `ranchu` or `vbox` |
| `product` | `Build.PRODUCT` contains `sdk`, `vbox86p` or `emulator` |
| `brand_device` | Both `Build.BRAND` and `Build.DEVICE` start with `generic` |
| `qemu_pipe` | `/dev/socket/qemud` or `/dev/qemu_pipe` exists |

`qemu_pipe` is strong. The six build-property signals are medium, so any two
of them together mean `detected`. The stock Android emulator (API 37, verified)
fires `hardware` and `product`.

These are build-property heuristics. They catch the stock Android emulator
and Genymotion reliably. Custom emulators that rewrite build properties may
not be detected.

## iOS signal

| Signal | Condition |
|---|---|
| `simulator_target` | The app was compiled for the Simulator (`targetEnvironment(simulator)`) |

This is exact rather than heuristic: it's decided at compile time, so it's
strong on the Simulator and never fires on a device.
